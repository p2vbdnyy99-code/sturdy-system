**Under Rule 170(i) of the General Financial Rules 2017, as revised on 25 July 2017, central government buyers should not ask for EMD from:**
- micro and small enterprises (MSEs);
- firms registered with the Central Purchase Organisation or the buying ministry;
- startups recognised by DPIIT.

On GeM there are two conditions. Only manufacturers of the goods or providers of the service qualify as MSEs, so traders do not. And the proof must be uploaded with the bid.

## What is EMD, and how much is it?

EMD (earnest money deposit), or bid security, protects the buyer if a bidder withdraws or changes its bid while the bid is valid.

- **Amount:** Rule 170(i) says it "should ordinarily range between two percent to five percent of the estimated value of the goods to be procured".
- **Validity:** it is normally valid for 45 days beyond the final bid validity period.
- **Accepted forms:** demand draft, fixed deposit receipt, banker's cheque, bank guarantee, or online payment.

## Who is exempt from EMD?

The revised Rule 170(i), in the Department of Expenditure's office memorandum No. F.20/2/2014-PPD(Pt.) of 25 July 2017, says bid security "is to be obtained from the bidders except":

| Exempt bidder | Condition in the rule |
|---|---|
| Micro and Small Enterprises | "as defined in MSE Procurement Policy issued by Department of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises" |
| Firms registered with the buyer | "registered with the Central Purchase Organisation or the concerned Ministry or Department" |
| Startups | "as recognised by Department of Industrial Policy & Promotion (DIPP)", now DPIIT |

Medium enterprises are not in the list.

## How does EMD exemption work on GeM?

A GeM bid states it in the bid document. In GEM/2024/B/5263261 (7 August 2024), for example:

> "The bidder seeking EMD exemption, must submit the valid supporting document for the relevant category as per GeM GTC with the bid. Under MSE category, only manufacturers for goods and Service Providers for Services are eligible for exemption from EMD. Traders are excluded from the purview of this Policy."

The same bid's disclaimer lists clauses a buyer must not add. One of them is "seeking EMD submission from bidder(s), including via Additional Terms & Conditions, in contravention to exemption provided to such sellers under GeM GTC". If a buyer adds such a clause, "the bid and resultant contracts shall be treated as null and void and such bids may be cancelled by GeM".

### How to claim it on GeM, step by step

1. **Check your role.** Are you the manufacturer of the goods, or the provider of the service? If you resell another company's product, the MSE EMD exemption doesn't apply to you.
2. **Check your certificate.**
   - For an MSE: a valid Udyam registration. GeM also says MSE benefits apply only if the seller is validated in its GeM profile.
   - For a startup: the DPIIT recognition certificate.
3. **Upload the proof with the bid,** in the document section the bid asks for. Without it, the buyer can treat the bid as having no EMD.
4. **Read the bid's EMD and ATC sections** for anything specific to that bid, and any corrigendum.

## Is a bid security declaration the same as an EMD exemption?

No.
- **A declaration replaces the deposit.** It is a signed undertaking in place of money: if you withdraw or change your bid during validity, or don't sign the contract, you can be suspended from bidding for a period the tender states.
- **An exemption means no bid security at all.** It applies only to the categories above.

Some tenders ask everyone for a declaration instead of EMD; read what yours asks. A Department of Expenditure memorandum of 12 November 2020 told ministries to use declarations instead of bid security, but only for tenders issued up to 31 December 2021. Today, the tender's own terms decide.

## Common mistakes

- Claiming the MSE exemption as a trader or reseller.
- Uploading an expired or wrong-category certificate, or none.
- Assuming a medium enterprise is exempt.
- Assuming the exemption also waives the performance security (ePBG) after award. It doesn't; that is a separate requirement.
- Not checking the corrigendum, which can change the EMD amount.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are MSEs exempt from EMD in government tenders?**
Under GFR Rule 170(i), central government buyers should not take bid security from MSEs as defined in the MSE Procurement Policy. On GeM, only manufacturers of the goods or providers of the service qualify; traders don't.

**Are startups exempt from EMD?**
Startups recognised by DPIIT are exempt under the same rule. On GeM, upload the DPIIT recognition certificate with the bid.

**Are traders exempt from EMD on GeM?**
No. GeM bid documents state that traders are excluded from the MSE EMD exemption.

**Is a medium enterprise exempt from EMD?**
Not under Rule 170(i), which names micro and small enterprises only.

**Which document proves MSE status on GeM?**
A valid Udyam registration, validated in your GeM seller profile. Check the bid for any other document it names.

**Can a buyer still ask an exempt MSE for EMD?**
GeM bid documents say that if a buyer adds a clause asking exempt sellers for EMD, the bid and resulting contract are treated as null and void and the bid may be cancelled by GeM. Outside GeM, state government and PSU tenders follow their own rules; read the tender.

**Does the exemption apply to state government tenders?**
GFR applies to central government. State buyers and PSUs set their own procurement rules, and many give MSEs a similar exemption. Check each tender.

**How much is EMD usually?**
GFR Rule 170(i) says it should ordinarily be 2% to 5% of the estimated value of the goods. The tender states the exact amount.

**Is a bid security declaration the same as EMD exemption?**
No. A declaration is a signed undertaking instead of a deposit, with suspension if you break it. Exemption means no bid security is asked at all.

**Does EMD exemption cover performance security?**
No. Performance security (ePBG on GeM) is asked from the winning bidder after award and is separate from EMD.
