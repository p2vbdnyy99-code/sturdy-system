**To check eligibility in an Indian government tender, find its eligibility section and test your company against each condition in turn:**
- registration and class;
- turnover;
- similar-work experience;
- certificates;
- any exemption you claim.

Use the exact numbers, years and proof the tender asks for, then read every corrigendum, because corrigenda change eligibility too. The tender's own wording decides; two tenders for similar work can set different thresholds.

## Step 1: Find every place eligibility is written

Eligibility is rarely in one place. Look in:
- the notice inviting tender (NIT);
- the instructions to bidders;
- the "qualification" or "eligibility criteria" section;
- the bid data sheet (on GeM, the first pages of the bid document);
- the additional terms and conditions (ATC on GeM);
- every corrigendum.

Note the page of each condition so you can find it again.

## Step 2: Check registration and class

Works tenders often admit only contractors registered in a stated class with named departments.

**Example.** An All India Radio civil construction tender in CPWD format invites bids from "approved and eligible contractor of CPWD / MES / BSNL / MTNL / Railway / West Bengal State PWD".

If your registration isn't on the list, you are not eligible, however strong your experience.

## Step 3: Check the turnover clause in four parts

A turnover clause can fail you in four ways. Answer each one:

1. **Which years?** The last three financial years, three of the last five, or another window.
2. **Average or every year?** An average lets one weak year pass. "In each year" does not.
3. **Whole company or the tendered item?** Some tenders count only turnover from the item or service being bought.
4. **What proof?** Audited statements, a chartered accountant's certificate in a set format, or both.

Compare your audited figures, not your estimates, against the exact percentage of the estimated cost the tender sets.

## Step 4: Check similar-work experience: count, value, window

Experience clauses usually let you qualify in one of three ways, and the numbers differ between tenders:

| Tender | Three similar works, each at least | Two, each at least | One, at least | Window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All India Radio civil works tender (CPWD format) | 40% of estimated cost | 60% | 80% | Last 7 years |
| GeM services bid GEM/2024/B/5263261 (07-08-2024) | 40% | 50% | 80% | Current financial year and last 3 (ending March before bid opening) |

Two details decide many bids:
- **Older works can be brought up to today's value.** The All India Radio tender raises the value of past work "at simple rate of 7 % per annum" from completion to the last date of application. Not every tender allows this.
- **"Similar work" is defined, and the definition can change.** One IIT Kanpur corrigendum of 5 May 2026 changed "similar work" from renovation and maintenance work to construction work in residential and office buildings (see [what a corrigendum can change](/guides/tender-corrigendum)).

## Step 5: Check exemptions before you rule yourself out

On GeM, a buyer can exempt micro and small enterprises (MSEs) and startups from the turnover and experience criteria.

- **Where to look:** the bid's first page shows "MSE Exemption for Years Of Experience and Turnover" and "Startup Exemption …" as Yes or No.
- **Example:** in GEM/2024/B/5263261 both are "Yes".
- **The condition:** the exemption is "subject to meeting of quality and technical specifications".
- **The proof:** the documents proving the exemption "must be uploaded for evaluation by the buyer".

EMD exemption is a separate rule: see [EMD exemption for MSEs and startups](/guides/emd-exemption).

## Step 6: List the proof for each condition

For each condition, write down the exact document you will upload:
- registration certificate;
- audited balance sheets;
- CA certificate;
- completion certificates or work orders;
- Udyam or DPIIT certificate.

Check that each is valid on the bid's closing date. Missing or expired proof fails a bid as surely as a missed threshold.

## Step 7: Read every corrigendum, then decide

Corrigenda change thresholds, registration lists and definitions after you have read the tender. Check for them the day before you submit, and re-test any condition they touch.

## Common mistakes

- Using the turnover of the wrong years, or an average where "each year" is required.
- Counting ongoing or unrelated work as "similar work".
- Missing that the two-works tier is 60%, not 50% (or the other way round).
- Claiming an MSE or startup exemption the bid doesn't allow, or without uploading proof.
- Checking the original tender but not the corrigendum.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is eligibility criteria in a tender?**
The conditions a bidder must meet for its bid to be considered: usually registration, turnover, similar-work experience, certificates and sometimes location or manufacturer status. They are set in the tender document and its corrigenda.

**How is average annual turnover calculated for a tender?**
Add the turnover for the financial years the tender names and divide by their number, using audited figures. Check whether the tender wants an average or a minimum in each year, and whether only turnover from the tendered item counts.

**What counts as "similar work"?**
Whatever the tender's definition says. Look for a "definition of similar work" clause; it can be narrow, such as only maintenance or only construction, and a corrigendum can change it.

**Can older completed works count at today's value?**
Only if the tender says so. Some CPWD-format tenders increase past work value at a simple 7% a year to the last date of application.

**Are MSEs exempt from turnover and experience criteria?**
On GeM, only if the bid says "Yes" to the MSE exemption, and only subject to meeting quality and technical specifications, with proof uploaded. Outside GeM, check the tender.

**Are startups exempt from experience criteria?**
On GeM, when the bid shows "Startup Exemption … Yes". Upload your DPIIT recognition as proof.

**What if I meet the experience in a joint venture?**
Check the tender's joint-venture or consortium clause; many say how each member's share counts, and some don't allow JVs at all.

**Can I bid if I meet everything except one condition?**
Usually the bid will be rejected on that condition. Ask in the pre-bid meeting or by the query date if the clause is unclear; answers usually come in writing as a corrigendum or minutes.

**How long does it take to check eligibility?**
For a long tender, finding every condition is the slow part. A tool that lists them with their pages, such as TenderTez, cuts that to minutes; you still verify each one.

**Where do I find corrigenda?**
On the tender's page on the portal where it was published, and on the buyer's website if the notice says so.
