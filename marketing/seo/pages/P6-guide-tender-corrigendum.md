**A corrigendum is an official amendment to a published tender.** It can change any part of it, including the closing date, the EMD, the eligibility conditions, the scope or a definition. Before you submit, find every corrigendum issued for the tender, read the changed clause in full, and re-check your bid against it. Never rely only on the notice's one-line summary.

## What can a corrigendum change?

Anything in the tender, for example:
- the bid closing or opening date;
- the EMD amount or its exemption terms;
- eligibility: registrations accepted, turnover, experience;
- the definition of "similar work";
- the scope, quantities or BOQ;
- the documents to be uploaded.

A corrigendum may also change nothing about the date. Don't assume a new corrigendum means more time.

## A real example: a corrigendum that changed who is eligible

A corrigendum issued by IIT Kanpur's Institute Works Department on 5 May 2026, for an annual civil maintenance contract (NIT 06/Civil/D2/2025-26), made four changes, each shown as "Content" and "Read As" with the tender page it amends:

| What changed | Original (as the corrigendum quotes it) | Read as |
|---|---|---|
| Registration accepted (pages 5, 20, 28) | CPWD, BSNL, MES, UPPWD, Central PSUs | CPWD / BSNL / MES / Central Government bodies / Central Government PSUs / State Government bodies |
| Eligible contractors (page 21, point 3.1) | Same named list | Same widened list |
| Experience with government (page 21) | One completed similar work of at least 40% of the estimated cost with a central department, central autonomous body, central PSU or "State Government Department" in the last 7 years | The same 40%, now "either part of above or a separate one", with "State Government bodies" |
| Definition of similar work (page 21) | "Renovation / Annual repairs & maintenance of works in residential houses / apartments / quarters / offices / laboratories" | "Construction works in residential houses / apartments / quarters / offices / laboratories" |

It ends: "The others terms & condition of the NIT shall remain the same."

**What this means for a bidder.** A contractor registered with a state government body became eligible, and one with only repair-and-maintenance experience may no longer have matching "similar work". Neither change was visible in the original tender.

## How to check for corrigenda

1. **The day you download the tender,** note where it was published: CPPP, GeM, a state e-procurement portal or the buyer's own site.
2. **Before you start preparing,** check that page for corrigenda, addenda and pre-bid minutes.
3. **The day before you submit,** check again. Late corrigenda are common.
4. **Download each corrigendum** and keep it with the tender. Some tenders ask you to sign and upload corrigenda with your bid; check yours.

## How to check what a corrigendum changed

1. **List each change** with the clause and page it amends. Most corrigenda quote "content" and "read as"; some only say "refer to the attached document".
2. **Re-check your eligibility** against any changed condition. See [how to check eligibility](/guides/tender-eligibility-check).
3. **Update your checklist** of documents to upload.
4. **Update the dates** in your plan if the closing date moved, and arrange the EMD again if its amount or validity changed.
5. **Keep the original.** If the corrigendum and the tender conflict, the corrigendum is the later instruction; if anything is unclear, ask the buyer in writing.

## Common mistakes

- Reading the notice summary ("date extended") and missing a changed eligibility clause in the attachment.
- Checking for corrigenda once, early, and missing a late one.
- Updating the date but not the checklist.
- Not uploading signed corrigenda when the tender asks for them.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a corrigendum in a tender?**
An official amendment issued after a tender is published, correcting or changing part of it: dates, EMD, eligibility, scope, definitions or documents.

**What is the difference between a corrigendum and an addendum?**
Tenders use the words loosely. Both amend the tender; "addendum" often adds material, and "corrigendum" often corrects it. Treat both as part of the tender.

**Where do I find a tender's corrigendum?**
On the tender's page on the portal where it was published (CPPP, GeM or the state e-procurement portal), and on the buyer's website if the notice says so.

**Does a corrigendum always extend the deadline?**
No. Many corrigenda change other terms and keep the original closing date.

**Can a corrigendum change eligibility after I have started preparing?**
Yes. The IIT Kanpur example above widened the registrations accepted and changed the definition of similar work.

**Do I need to upload the corrigendum with my bid?**
Some tenders ask for signed corrigenda with the bid. Check the tender and each corrigendum.

**What if a corrigendum is unclear?**
Ask the buyer in writing before the query deadline. Answers usually come as a further corrigendum or pre-bid minutes.

**Can I bid on the original terms if I missed a corrigendum?**
No. The amended terms apply to every bidder, whether or not they read the corrigendum.
