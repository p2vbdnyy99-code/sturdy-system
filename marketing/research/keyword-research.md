# Keyword research

**No search-volume data.** We have no keyword tool, and Search Console's query
data (free) is not connected yet. The seed terms below are unverified
hypotheses. What we checked on 16 Oct is who appears for them in web search.

| Seed term | Intent | What ranks (16 Oct search) | Our page |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI tender analysis India | Find a tool | Software directories (GetApp, Capterra) listing Tenders Agent; BidShakti profiles | `/`, `/features` |
| tender document summary / summarisation | Find a tool | Same tool listings | `/` |
| tender eligibility checker | Find a tool / check a tender | Tool pages, buyers' eligibility PDFs | `/features` |
| how to check eligibility in a tender (turnover, experience) | Learn | Tender PDFs, corrigenda, a startup guide (echai.ventures), court orders | **None: an article opportunity** |
| EMD exemption MSE / GeM | Learn | Buyers' bid documents, DoE memorandum, policy summaries | **None: an article opportunity** |
| government tenders in <state> / <organisation> tenders | Find tenders | Aggregators (Tender18, TendersOnTime, BidAssist) | 46 landing pages |
| tender consultant software | Find a tool | not checked yet | `/features` (client workspaces) |

## What this suggests

1. Tool-intent terms are dominated by directories. Listing TenderTez on free
   software directories (GetApp/Capterra free listings, if still free) is a
   legitimate way to appear there; check their terms first.
2. Learning terms ("how to check eligibility", "EMD exemption") have no strong
   explainer pages; answers sit inside PDFs. A few careful, sourced articles
   would be genuinely useful. They need a blog section on the site (a website
   change: founder approval).
3. Tender-finding terms are crowded by aggregators; our landing pages compete
   on a real, current list with portal links.
